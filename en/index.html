  1. SignalA local sensor you switched on reports a real event — a file, an appointment, a message.
  2. MemoryHunch links it to a goal, an earlier decision or an open loop.
  3. Recognised intentThe Observer estimates a possible intent and rates its confidence — and may decide to do nothing.
  4. Quality thresholdOnly sufficient relevance (default 0.85) moves things forward. Anything below is filed quietly.
  5. Thread or actionFor a longer task Hunch starts a thread; otherwise the Executive prepares the next step.
  6. Authority GateBefore a risky action Hunch shows reason, target, tool and concrete arguments — you approve or decline.
  7. ResultAfter approval the Executive acts, verifies the result and delivers it to the active surface.
  8. LearnedThe outcome improves Hunch's personal knowledge — or is saved as a reusable routine.

Ordinary assistants

  • wait for a prompt
  • start from scratch in every new chat
  • mostly know the current conversation
  • need to be told again and again how to work
  • process tasks one after another
  • tie identity and features to one vendor
  • treat autonomy like an on/off switch

Hunch

  • also reacts to relevant changes, not only to prompts
  • has a lasting identity and relationship with you
  • connects memory, goals, patterns, working style and context
  • remembers how you want to be addressed and supported
  • pursues several threads in parallel
  • is separate from the interchangeable model
  • distinguishes perceiving, proposing and acting — and is explicitly allowed to do nothing

A good intentional agent is not as active as possible. It also recognises when no intervention makes sense.

Personal model & anticipation

What makes Hunch anticipate.

The personal model connects only activated, controllable sources — among them memories, conversations, tasks, goals and intentions, decisions, people and topics, working rhythms, apps, files and projects in use, recurring routines, open loops and the results of earlier actions. “Hunch reads everything” is explicitly wrong — browser, clipboard, file, app and screen context are optional, locally controllable sensors.

Threads

You keep thinking, Hunch keeps working.

Threads are parallel, persistent lines of work within your relationship with Hunch. While a longer task runs, the main conversation stays free immediately — an ordinary assistant would block the dialogue. Every thread has a clear goal, its own context, its own tool rounds, approvals and a result that returns to the original channel.

IdentityToday

The owner device creates a private key in the Secure Enclave — biometrically protected, not exportable, not in backups. Your HID is derived from the public key.

Authority & riskToday

The Governor distinguishes risk classes, budgets, limits, always-ask rules and circuit breakers.

Second opinionToday

A separate review model may only rate concrete, non-critical steps as harmless. Critical actions are never approved automatically.

Preview & consentToday

Before protected actions you see tool, arguments, target, risk and reasoning. You approve or decline.

Execution & auditToday

Action, decision, result and cost remain traceable in the append-only log.

IsolationToday

Guests get a separate context — neither your personal memory nor your tools.

Impulse & failure brakeToday

Minimum spacing plus a daily limit for impulses; three failures of one tool lead to a pause.

Way back & boundaryToday

Remembered facts and created tasks can be taken back; the boundary — what Hunch never does without asking — is visible in one place. Full undo for every action and finer device trust are still being built.Next

Hunch may perceive, formulate an intent and propose something — but act only with matching authority. Deleting, buying, sending externally and publishing are specially protected.
Before an owner identity is set up, a less strict mode can exist for compatibility. The cryptographic lock is not automatically active before setup is complete.

Local & your hardware

Personal memory lives locally by default. The runtime runs on your own hardware.

Sensors opt-in

Sensors are activated explicitly, imports are opt-in. No “Hunch reads everything”.

Isolation & authority

Guests are isolated, critical tools need authority, external agents only get limited interfaces.

Foreign content = distrust

Foreign content is treated as potentially untrustworthy and handled as a quote, never as a command.

Visible & deletable

Memories, preferences and learned skills are visible, controllable and deletable.
